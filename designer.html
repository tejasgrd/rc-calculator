Advanced: Aircraft Designer

Design a plane from your requirements

Tell it the mission, size and stall speed. The designer then works out the airframe and the weight breakdown, and checks stability and performance with real (simplified) aerodynamics. It also matches a motor and prop, shows the plane in 3D, and hands everything to the Build page for printable templates.

Requirements→Size→Check stability & performance→Pick power system→3D→Build templates→Fly it

Engineering estimates. The models are classical and simplified: weight build-up, Helmbold lift slope, tail-volume neutral point, Prandtl lifting line, a drag build-up, and a motor-constants model. They are useful for comparing options and catching mistakes. They do not replace XFLR5, eCalc, a watt meter, or a careful CG check before the first flight.

    Control surfaces ?
          
    Chord % is the share of the local chord that moves; span % is measured from the wing root (0) to the tip (100). These drive the drawings, templates, 3D model and simulator.
    
          
    
          
          
    
          
    
        
    
        
    Motor mount (thrust line) ?
          
    Tilting the motor stops the plane pitching or swinging when you change power. Down-thrust points the thrust slightly below the fuselage line; right-thrust points it towards the right wing. Look at the prop from behind the plane to see which way it spins.

      As built (measured) Physics
      
      
      Weigh the finished plane and find its balance point (support it under the wing at equal distances from the fuselage). Enter the measurements here: the stability analysis, performance, 3D model and simulator then use your real plane instead of the estimate.
      
      
      
        
        
      
        
      
      
      
      
      
    
      
    
      
      
      Multi-panel wing CG calculator Physics
      
      
      For wings with several panels (for example a straight centre section with tapered or swept tips): integrates the planform for area, MAC and its position, then puts the CG at the target static margin. Neutral point is at the MAC quarter-chord (exact for a tailless wing; a tail moves it aft).
      
      
      
      
      
      
      
      
      
      
      
    
      
  
      

      



      
  
      
    
      5Wing analysis (lifting line)
      Prandtl lifting-line theory solves the spanwise lift on your tapered, twisted wing. It shows how close the wing is to ideal (elliptic) loading and where the stall starts: tip stall causes a wing drop, root stall is gentle. Sweep is not modelled.
      
    
      
    
      
      
      Spanwise lift distribution at cruise
      Normalised load cl·c / (CL·cmean) compared with the ideal elliptic shape.
      
      
      Local cl ÷ clmax at the onset of stall
      The first point to touch 1.0 stalls first. Add washout or reduce taper if this is near the tip.

      7Power system matching
      An eCalc-style motor model (Kv, winding resistance Rm, no-load current Io, battery resistance) finds the real operating point of each motor and prop combination. It keeps the ones inside the motor's limits and ranks them by how well they fit your plane.
